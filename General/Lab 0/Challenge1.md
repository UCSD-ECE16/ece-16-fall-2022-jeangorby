# Challenge 1: Design Thinking 

## Identifying a user of interest
- Undergraduate STEM students who are required to take GE courses in humanities and/or history

## Identifying their task
- Reading long texts not in the best of their interests
- Writing essays in formats that they are not interested

## Questions about their task
- Why are these humanities texts not interesting to STEM students?
- Are there cases where an undergrad STEM student's GPA has been significantly impacted because of these GE's?
- Does this have to do with time management or is it entirely due to lesser interests in the topic?

## Answers to these questions
- STEM students often only want to get hands on with their major rather than deviating away into history which is a topic that many students dread.
- History and humanities texts are often so long that their motivation is decreased because they would much rather spend their time on STEM related activities.
- There are people who's major GPA is better than their overall GPA because the major GPA only considers classes that are well within this STEM range.
- It has to do with both time management and less interests. The lesser the interests a person has on a subject, the more likely they are to spend more time in this activity being unproductive.

## Problem Statement
After enrolling into college for a STEM major, undergrads are more often than not required to take GE courses in either humanities and/or history. This massive departure from the major they applied into often leads to these same students not putting in the same effort into these GE's because they are less interested. This leads to lower GPA's and even affects time management because they tend to be unproductive. In order to battle this problem, GE's could be adjusted in a way that maintains the interests of STEM students (for instance, a history in sciences rather than history of the US). Communities can also be established to connect STEM students to non-STEM students which can create conversation regarding the topics at hand.
